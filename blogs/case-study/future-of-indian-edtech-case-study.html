The Rise, Fall & Future of Online EdTech in India: Deep Dive Case Study

How bloated marketing budgets collapsed early unicorns, and why sustainable, teacher-first models like Mendorbit are winning student trust.

Between 2020 and 2022, India's EdTech ecosystem witnessed astronomical venture capital investment. Flashy celebrity endorsements, aggressive sales tactics, and multi-billion-dollar paper valuations dominated national headlines. Yet by 2024, multiple high-profile unicorns crumbled under the weight of customer acquisition costs, regulatory scrutiny, and plummeting renewal rates.

At Mendorbit, where we prioritize student learning outcomes above all else, we conducted an exhaustive investigation into why the first wave of Indian EdTech failed grassroots learners—and what the next generation of sustainable, educator-centric platforms must do differently.

1. The Pandemic Gold Rush & The Illusion of Infinite Growth (2020–2022)

When physical classrooms abruptly shuttered in March 2020, millions of Indian households turned to mobile screens for continuity. Venture capital poured over \.5 Billion into Indian educational startups in less than 24 months. However, instead of deploying this capital into pedagogical research, teacher training, or lower tuition fees, early unicorns treated education as high-frequency software sales:

  • Bloated Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC): Companies spent as much as 60% to 75% of their total revenue on television commercials, sports jersey sponsorships, and aggressive digital ad bidding.
  • Outsourced Sales Fleets: Business Development Associates (BDAs) were incentivized via aggressive commissions to sell expensive 3-year subscription packages rather than guiding students toward the right course.
  • Neglect of the Learning Curve: Technology was treated as a replacement for human educators, leaving students with pre-recorded videos that offered zero emotional connection or real-time doubt clearing.

2. The Structural Flaws: Why Completion Rates Collapsed Below 8%

Education is fundamentally distinct from consumer internet businesses like e-commerce or food delivery. While ordering food requires zero effort from the user, learning requires discipline, discomfort, problem-solving, and continuous motivation. When platforms automated everything into passive video libraries, students simply stopped logging in:

Predatory NBFC Financing

Low-income families were enrolled into third-party auto-debit loans disguised as 'free trial EMIs', triggering immense psychological distress when children wanted to cancel courses.

Zero Accountability for Doubts

When a Class 10 student struggled with Quadratic Equations or a CLAT aspirant got stuck in Legal Reasoning, bot-based automated responses failed to address the root cognitive block.

4. The Horizon: How Honest Education Wins Over Marketing

The future of Indian education does not belong to trillion-dollar tech conglomerates selling tablets. It belongs to authentic educators with high subject authority who leverage modern digital infrastructure to eliminate geographic barriers without inflating costs.

As our founding philosophy at Mendorbit states: "हम टॉपर्स नहीं चुनते, हम टॉपर्स बनाते हैं!" When a platform treats every student as a future achiever rather than a sales target, real academic transformations occur.

5. Frequently Asked Questions (EdTech Ecosystem FAQs)

Q: Are online classes genuinely effective for Class 10 Board preparation?

Yes, provided the classes are live, interactive, and coupled with daily handwritten notes and weekly NCERT problem drills rather than passive recorded video watching.

Q: What should parents check before enrolling in any online educational platform?

Always check who the actual teachers are (credentials, past teaching lectures), whether there is a transparent refund policy, and ensure the platform never pushes third-party loan agreements.
