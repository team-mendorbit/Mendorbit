Constitution Day 2026: 15 Most Repeated Articles, Preamble & Amendments Cheat-Sheet for CLAT & SSC

Every year on 26 November, India celebrates Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas) — the day in 1949 when the Constituent Assembly formally adopted the Constitution of India (before it came into full force on 26 January 1950). Today, 26 November 2026, marks the 77th anniversary of that historic adoption, celebrated nationwide under themes like "Hamara Samvidhan, Hamara Swabhiman" (Our Constitution, Our Pride).

Zero-Fluff Exam Reality

Most articles you read today will give you a generic history lesson or a school speech. Not here. As someone who has sat through intense CLAT preparation and now builds Mendorbit while studying at IIT Patna, I know one hard truth about competitive exams: you don't lose marks because you didn't read the Constitution — you lose marks because you couldn't recall the exact Article or Amendment under exam pressure.

With CLAT 2027 just days away (6 December 2026) and SSC Tier 2 / upcoming cycles around the corner, here is your crisp Constitution Day Revision Cheat-Sheet.

1. The Preamble: 4 Facts Examiners Never Get Tired Of Asking

Before jumping into Articles, lock these four Preamble essentials in your memory:

1
Adopted Date mentioned in the Preamble: 26th day of November, 1949 (often tested directly in SSC & CLAT GK).
2
The 5 Core Nature Keywords (in exact order): Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic.
3
The Only Amendment to the Preamble: 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 — added 3 words: Socialist, Secular, and Integrity.
4
Key Supreme Court Ruling: In Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973), the Supreme Court held that the Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution and forms part of its Basic Structure.

2. The 15 Most Repeated Articles for CLAT & SSC

If you only have 10 minutes to revise Indian Polity before an exam, revise these 15 Articles:

Article Provision Why Examiners Love Asking This
Article 14 Equality before Law & Equal Protection of Laws Foundation of Rule of Law (A.V. Dicey concept).
Article 17 Abolition of Untouchability Absolute right — no exceptions, punishable by law.
Article 19 Six Fundamental Freedoms (Speech, Assembly, Association, Movement, Residence, Profession) Frequently paired with Reasonable Restrictions under Art 19(2) in CLAT passages.
Article 21 Protection of Life and Personal Liberty Widest scope! Includes Right to Privacy (K.S. Puttaswamy case) & Speedy Trial.
Article 21A Right to Free & Compulsory Education (ages 6–14) Added by the 86th Amendment Act, 2002.
Article 32 Right to Constitutional Remedies (5 Writs) Called the "Heart and Soul of the Constitution" by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.
Article 40 Organization of Village Panchayats Classic Gandhian DPSP question in SSC Prelims.
Article 44 Uniform Civil Code (UCC) High-frequency current affairs + Legal Reasoning overlap.
Article 51A Fundamental Duties (11 Duties) Added by 42nd Amendment (1976) on Swaran Singh Committee recommendation; 11th duty added by 86th Amendment (2002).
Article 72 Pardoning Power of the President Compare with Article 161 (Governor's pardoning power — Governor cannot pardon a death sentence).
Article 110 Definition of Money Bill Lok Sabha Speaker's certification on whether a bill is a Money Bill is final.
Article 123 Ordinance-Making Power of the President Compare with Article 213 (Governor's ordinance power).
Article 226 Writ Jurisdiction of High Courts Wider than Article 32 — covers both Fundamental Rights AND other legal rights.
Article 280 Finance Commission Constituted by the President every 5 years.
Art 352 / 356 / 360 Emergency Provisions 352: National Emergency | 356: President's Rule | 360: Financial Emergency (never imposed in India so far).

3. Bonus: Article 368 & The "Mini Constitution" Amendments

In CLAT Legal Reasoning and SSC General Awareness, Amendments are almost always tested alongside Articles. Memorize these 7 High-Yield Amendments:

42nd (1976)
"Mini Constitution": Added Socialist, Secular, Integrity to the Preamble + added Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Art 51A).
44th (1978)
Removed Right to Property from Fundamental Rights and made it a legal right under Article 300A; replaced "Internal Disturbance" with "Armed Rebellion" for National Emergency.
61st (1988)
Lowered the voting age from 21 years to 18 years.
73rd & 74th
(1992): Constitutional status to Panchayati Raj (11th Schedule) and Municipalities (12th Schedule).
86th (2002)
Added Article 21A (Right to Education) and the 11th Fundamental Duty.
101st (2016)
Introduced GST (Goods and Services Tax).
106th (2023)
Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam: 33% reservation for women in Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies.

Remember: Parliament's power to amend the Constitution comes from Article 368, but it cannot alter the Basic Structure.

4. A Quick 2-Minute Constitution Day Action Plan

Instead of just forwarding a "Happy Constitution Day" status today, do this:

CLAT 2027 Aspirants

Bookmark this table and revise Articles 14, 19, 21, 32, 226, and 368 tonight.

SSC & Banking Aspirants

Memorize the 7 Amendments and Articles 40, 44, 51A, 110, 280, and 352–360 — these show up in almost every exam cycle.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the most commonly asked Constitution Day & Indian Polity exam questions.

Because on 26 November 1949, the Constituent Assembly of India formally adopted the Constitution of India. It came into effect two months later on 26 January 1950 (Republic Day).
Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) was called the "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar because it empowers citizens to move the Supreme Court directly if their Fundamental Rights are violated.
Under Article 32, the Supreme Court issues writs only for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights. Under Article 226, High Courts have a wider jurisdiction — they can issue writs for Fundamental Rights as well as other ordinary legal rights.
